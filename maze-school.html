Rimewall · simulation findings

Maze School

252 simulated matches set out to find the best maze for each race. They found something more urgent: mazing does not currently pay.

01 The finding that changes the plan

One maze. Built from the gate outward. Every stage defends while it grows.

The ceiling is a weave of both corridors and the band: 202 towers, west route 171 against a gate pile's 92. It is the target, and the wave budget is simply how far along it you have got.

Of five orderings of those same 202 cells, building nearest the ship outward beats every alternative — 71.2 mean lives from wave 15 against 27.9 for building the corridors first. Same maze, same cells, 2.5x the result.

Arm the path you have before extending it. Corridor towers divert nothing until enough of them exist, and until then creeps stroll past to an undefended gate.

The same design, at the gold each wave provides

Every frame is a prefix of the one after it. Nothing is rebuilt or sold; each wave's income extends the maze one wall further from the ship.

03 Revised plan

The teaching feature is still right. It cannot ship first, because the lesson it teaches is currently false.

  1. Widen the difficulty band — this is the one that unblocks everything. The game currently jumps from trivial to unwinnable with nothing in between, so no build choice can matter. Success looks like a stretch of waves where a 50-tower build survives and a 23-tower build does not. Everything else waits on this.
  2. Then re-measure maze against cluster inside that band. If the extra 9 steps of path decide matches there, mazing pays and the teaching feature is sound as designed. If it still does not, mazing needs a direct mechanical reward — a damage or bounty scalar on route length — and that is a design decision, not something the harness can settle.
  3. Fix the starting race. Independent of all the above: ice is the default and the seventh-weakest of nine at 98.5 mean. Retune it or start new players on tech or fire.
  4. Only then the pattern library and tooltips. The shapes are ready — zigzag-g4-l13 for teaching, zigzag-g3-l13 once past the tutorial — but they teach a lesson that is not yet true.
Do not start with the map. Splitting the flags was my first recommendation and the experiment disproved it: it made every build worse and changed no ranking. ROUTES is fine.

04 Reading these numbers honestly

  • One seed. The cluster-versus-maze gap at 1100g (56.4 vs 72.9) is wide enough to trust; individual per-race cells are not.
  • Tier 1 only. This compares kits at a fixed tier, not across the upgrade curve. A race that comes alive at tier 4 is undersold here.
  • No economy play. Fixed budget, no sending, no shrine progression — races that scale through income are undersold too.
  • Enemy sends were near-irrelevant. An earlier matrix with a scripted send every 30s moved lives lost by 0–7 in almost every cell. Send pressure is weak against the neutral ladder, which is its own question.
  • The cluster is one specific shape — 23 towers at rows 40–42, cols 11–20. A different cluster position would perform differently; this one sits on the funnel deliberately.
  • The biggest gap: nothing upgrades. Every run holds tier 1 for its whole duration and spends no income. Real play tiers up, and that may be exactly where mazing earns its keep — a maze's value scales with time-in-range, which compounds with damage upgrades in a way a fixed-tier run cannot show. Before concluding that mazing is worthless, the harness should be taught to buy upgrades. That is the next change I would make to it.
What I got wrong, in order. First I reported five hand-authored shapes as if comparing them were a search. Then I named the shared middle flag as the cause and proposed splitting it — the experiment disproved that. Then I proposed tight folds — disproved too. The surviving claim is narrow and worth stating plainly: in every configuration tested, at tier 1 with no upgrades, no maze beat a cluster on the gate funnel. That is a real result, and it is not the same as "mazing is bad".
